Section I

Welcome & Historical Context

You command a boat of the Kaiserliche Marine in a war fought as much over shipping as over territory.

Britain used sea power to restrict supplies reaching Germany. Germany sought to cut Britain’s own supply lines through submarine attacks on commerce. The campaign put merchant crews, passengers, and neutral shipping at risk. Disputes over these attacks helped widen the war.

1914
Britain begins its naval blockade of Germany. German submarines show they can threaten warships as well as trade routes.
1915-1916
Germany declares a war zone around the British Isles in 1915. Attacks on merchant and passenger vessels bring diplomatic protests and changes in restrictions.
1917
Germany resumes unrestricted submarine warfare in February. The United States enters the war in April. Britain expands escorted convoys to protect ocean traffic.
1918
Convoys and escorts continue to guard sea lanes. The armistice of 11 November ends the fighting.

First watch

Read the weather, check diesel, battery, and compressed air, then plot a course in open water. Observe a contact before selecting a weapon. Keep a route away from the engagement and leave enough reserve to dive or surface afterward.

Patrol records

Use Captain’s Log to review tonnage, crew experience, and patrol history. Save or load your campaign there. When planning the next patrol, the departure year governs which boats, torpedoes, and vessels are available.

Section II

Chart Room & Dead Reckoning

Plot a course with room to turn. Estimate your next position from heading, speed, and elapsed patrol time.

Plot and follow the course

  1. Left-click open water to append each waypoint. Clicking a visible contact instead selects it for combat.
  2. Set the engine telegraph. The boat turns gradually toward the next waypoint and stops when the last leg is complete.
  3. Right-click the chart to clear waypoints. Order Stop separately if you also want to halt.

Mind the reserves

Diesel powers surface movement and recharges the battery while underway. Submerged movement draws battery power. Slow or Half conserves reserves when there is time to maneuver.

Use 1× near land, contacts, or damage. Use 10× or 50× on clear legs. Time compression advances patrol time, including resource use and threats.

Dead reckoning example

Eight knots for 15 simulated minutes covers about two nautical miles. Advance the last fix along the boat’s actual heading. Recheck after turns and whenever the chart shows a speed change.

Dashed shipping lanes indicate common traffic routes. A War Zone marking identifies a patrol sector. Hover over chart contacts to inspect them before closing.

Section III

Conning Tower & Periscope Gunnery

Open Periscope or press Space. Use binoculars at 0 m and the periscope at 10-14 m. Order 12 m to observe and launch submerged.

  1. Identify and range. Pan with A/D or drag. Use Z for 1.5× or 6× zoom. Check mast height and top speed in the Target Recognition Manual. Estimate range in meters as mast height × 1,000 ÷ mils. A 24 m mast spanning 16 mils is about 1,500 m away.
  2. Enter the firing solution. Center the contact and enter estimated range, target speed in knots, and AOB. Enter 0° bow-on, ±90° broadside, or ±180° stern-on. Use positive angles when you see the target’s starboard side and negative when you see its port side.
  3. Check the gyro and fire. The Torpedo Data Computer leads the target and indicates a bow or stern tube. Steer the boat if the solution lies more than 30° off that tube’s axis. Allow about 300 m for arming, and wait for the impact report before spending another torpedo.

Section IV

Surface Raiders & Cruiser Rules

Surface fire saves torpedoes, but a boat at 0 m is exposed to return fire. Identify the ship and its escorts before closing.

Use the deck gun

At the surface, select a visible contact in Tactical mode or center it in the conning tower and use Fire Deck Gun. Range and weather affect accuracy. Check shell supply, reload, and target range before each shot.

Class and gunMax range
Type U-31 · 88 mm4,800 m
Type U-57 · 105 mm6,500 m

Claim a prize

  1. Surface and select an unarmed merchant in the chart’s fire-control panel.
  2. Use Warning shot. If the vessel stops and surrenders, close within 2 NM while it remains visible.
  3. Choose Claim tonnage. Check the flag first. Claiming or sinking a neutral incurs a diplomatic penalty.

Break contact

Treat an apparent steamer as unidentified until you have a safe approach. When hidden armament is revealed, order a dive, change course, and watch for escorts. Ordinary armed merchants can also return fire.

Historical cruiser rules called for stopping and inspecting merchantmen and providing for the safety of crews and passengers before destruction, subject to rules about escape or resistance. The game represents a prize through surrender and tonnage credit.

Section V

Evasion & Damage Control

Break the enemy’s line of contact, establish trim, and check the Control Room before committing to a second attack.

When an escort closes

Escorts drop depth charges near their last fix on your boat. Turn away after diving. Below the optical band, use Scan with fitted hydrophones. Listen at lower speed when safe. Self-noise, storms, and station damage cut listening range.

Use the class limits on the depth gauge. Beyond test depth, the hull creaks and takes damage. At crush depth the boat is lost. A Type U-31 tests at 50 m and crushes at 85 m.

Send the repair parties

Press C. Select one of four squads, then click a compartment. Parties drain water and repair machinery over time. Rotate tired squads to Rest in Quarters. Pump Bilges drains water using battery power.

Flooding pulls a slow boat down. Make 5 kn to arrest the downward drift if the engines can deliver it. Damage also slows reload, listening, diving, or propulsion according to the affected room.

Chlorine or uncontrolled sinking

Seawater reaching the batteries while submerged produces chlorine. Green haze marks contaminated rooms, and exposure can incapacitate squads. Surface and use Ventilate Hull. If you cannot hold trim, B blows ballast, spends 25 percentage points of compressed air, and holds the surfaced boat for up to 90 simulated seconds.
